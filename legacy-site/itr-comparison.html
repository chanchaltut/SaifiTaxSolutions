AIS, Form 26AS and Form 16:
What Is the Difference?

When you file your Income Tax Return, you encounter these three crucial documents. Understanding the difference can help you check your income details properly and drastically reduce the chances of errors.

01

The 3 Tax Pillars Explained

All three documents contain tax-related information, but they serve completely different purposes and originate from different sources.

Form 16

A TDS certificate issued directly by your employer. If you are salaried and TDS is deducted, you receive this.

  • Salary paid
  • Tax deducted
  • Exemptions claimed

Form 26AS

A tax passbook available on the e-Filing portal showing mainly TDS and TCS reported by deductors.

  • Employer TDS
  • Bank TDS
  • TCS collected

AIS

The Annual Information Statement. It provides a massive, 360-degree view of your financial footprint.

  • Interest & Dividends
  • Stock market data
  • Property transactions
02

The Difference at a Glance

Document What It Mainly Shows Where It Comes From
Form 16 Salary details and TDS deducted by your employer. Your Employer
Form 26AS Mainly TDS and TCS-related tax information. Income Tax Department
AIS Broader financial and tax-related information (Stocks, Interest, Property). Income Tax Department

Rule of Thumb: Form 16 = Salary | Form 26AS = Tax Credits | AIS = All Financial Activity

03

What If There is a Mismatch?

One of the most crucial steps before filing your ITR is to review and reconcile your information. Do not simply copy every figure from one document without checking it against your actual records.

What if AIS does not match your actual income?

Suppose your AIS shows interest income of ₹50,000, but your actual bank records show ₹42,000. Do not ignore the difference, and do not immediately assume AIS is correct. Follow these steps:

1
Identify the Source

Check where the information came from (which bank or broker) and what transaction has been reported.

2
Check Your Records

Compare it with relevant documents like Bank statements, Interest certificates, and Broker statements.

3
Understand the Difference

It could be a reporting difference, duplicate entry, or incorrect PAN mapping by the bank.

4
Take Action

If incorrect, AIS provides a facility to submit feedback online. For complex errors, seek professional help.

A Simple Example: Rahul's ITR

Let's take the example of Rahul, a salaried employee who also trades stocks and has FDs.

  • In Form 16: Rahul checks his exact Salary and the TDS deducted by his HR.
  • In Form 26AS: He verifies that his employer actually deposited that TDS to the government.
  • In AIS: He discovers ₹15,000 Bank Interest and ₹40,000 Capital Gains from his stock broker, which he must add to his ITR to avoid notices.

05

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Form 16 the same as AIS?

No. Form 16 is a salary TDS certificate provided directly by your employer. AIS is a broader information statement generated by the Income Tax Department containing your overall financial data.

Is Form 26AS the same as AIS?

No. Form 26AS mainly contains TDS and TCS-related information, while AIS provides much broader information including property, stocks, and high-value transactions.

Should salaried employees check AIS?

Yes, absolutely. Salaried employees often have bank interest or mutual fund transactions apart from salary. Reviewing AIS helps identify these additional reported incomes to avoid mismatch notices.

What should I do if Form 16 and Form 26AS do not match?

If the TDS shown in Form 16 is not visible in Form 26AS, do not claim it immediately. Contact your employer or deductor to clarify why the TDS has not been reflected in the government portal.
